1. Core idea and membrane machinery

A neuron produces an electrical signal by changing the selective permeability of its plasma membrane to ions.

Nerve impulse: a self-propagating electrochemical change in membrane potential that travels along a neuron.

Leak channels

Remain open. K+ leak channels contribute strongly to the negative resting potential.

Voltage-gated channels

Open or inactivate when voltage changes. Na+ and K+ channels generate the action potential.

Ligand-gated channels

Open when a neurotransmitter binds. They produce postsynaptic graded potentials.

Mechanically gated channels

Open after stretch, pressure or distortion. They occur in many sensory receptors.

Na+/K+ pump

Uses ATP to move 3 Na+ out and 2 K+ in. It maintains, rather than directly creates, the rapid action potential.

Selective membrane

At rest, the membrane is far more permeable to K+ than Na+. Large negative proteins remain inside.

All-or-none law: a stimulus below threshold produces no action potential. Once threshold is reached, the neuron produces a complete action potential of standard amplitude. Stronger stimuli increase impulse frequency, not the size of each action potential.

3. Conduction along the axon

Continuous conduction

In an unmyelinated axon, Na+ entry at one segment creates local current. The adjacent segment reaches threshold and produces a new action potential. Each membrane segment becomes active in sequence.

The region behind the impulse remains refractory, so immediate backward excitation cannot occur.

Saltatory conduction

Myelin prevents major ion exchange across internodes. Local current spreads rapidly beneath the myelin and action potentials arise mainly at nodes of Ranvier.

The impulse appears to jump between nodes. This makes conduction faster and reduces the ion pumping required afterward.

4. Transmission from one neuron to another

Synapse: the functional junction where a presynaptic neuron communicates with a postsynaptic neuron, muscle cell or gland cell.

Chemical synapse: six sequential events

1. Action potential reaches terminal
2. Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open
3. Ca²⁺ triggers vesicle exocytosis
4. Neurotransmitter diffuses across cleft
5. Receptors activate ion channels
6. EPSP or IPSP develops

EPSP

An excitatory postsynaptic potential depolarizes the membrane toward threshold. Na+ entry commonly contributes. Multiple EPSPs can summate.

IPSP

An inhibitory postsynaptic potential moves or holds the membrane away from threshold. Cl− entry or K+ exit commonly contributes.

Chemical synapse

Usually one-way, shows a short synaptic delay, allows amplification and modification, and can be excitatory or inhibitory.

Electrical synapse

Gap junctions provide a low-resistance path between cells. Transmission is extremely fast, often bidirectional, and useful for synchronized activity.

Signal termination: enzymes break down transmitter, transporters remove it by reuptake, or molecules diffuse away. Rapid removal prevents continuous stimulation.
